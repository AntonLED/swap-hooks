\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The framework's evaluation layer runs every fee policy and a family of
static baselines over identical replayed market histories and scores the
liquidity provider (LP) against simply holding the deposited tokens.
The experiment plays two roles: it exercises the full
configure--deploy--replay path at scale, and it provides a controlled
case study of whether the supported dynamic policies improve LP
outcomes over a static fee under the declared market model.

\subsection{Evaluated Policies}
\label{sec:policies}

All policies share the fee box $[1,100]$~bps (fees are \texttt{uint24}
values in hundredths of a basis point), and every updated fee is
clamped to that box (left implicit below).
Directional fees are written $f^{0\to1}$
(selling token0) and $f^{1\to0}$; every fee starts at 30~bps unless
noted. Gas is the EVM-measured median per swap, including the 21k
intrinsic transaction cost. Table~\ref{tab:policies} summarizes each
policy's signal, update rule, and concrete parameters.

\texttt{DAHook}, \texttt{ABHook}, and \texttt{MEVChargeHook} are
endogenous (their oracle arguments are unused); \texttt{BAHook} and
\texttt{VolatilityHook} read two Chainlink-compatible feeds, which is
what their higher gas reflects.

\subsection{Simulated Market and Reproducibility}

One \emph{cell} of the experiment's factorial matrix, a
combination of fee policy, trading pair, day-window, and gas-price
scenario, replays a \emph{window} of 1{,}440 one-minute Binance
candles from 2024 against a Uniswap~v4 pool holding a 20M~USDT basket,
split equally by value into a full-range position. The pool opens at the external market price of the first
candle; a mock Chainlink-compatible feed tracks each candle's close; the
first 60 candles execute but are excluded from measurement. Three pairs
are studied: ETH/SHIB and ETH/USDC (volatile) and USDC/USDT (stable). Windows are stratified
by realized volatility into terciles (24 calm, 24 medium, and 24
stormy days per pair), so market regimes are represented by design.

Every cell is identified by the manifest of Eq.~\eqref{eq:manifest}
(configured contract's source hash, initial price and liquidity, a
fingerprint of the window's own candles, and the RNG seed), so any
change to a contract or input identifies exactly the affected cells.
Because every
swap executes in a real EVM (Foundry), gas is \emph{measured} rather
than assumed: a calibration run records each policy's median gas per
swap (76k for the static hook, 88--124k for the adaptive ones), and
these measured figures feed back into both traders' decisions below.
Fees follow v4 accounting exactly: they accrue to the position and do
not compound.

\subsection{Two-Flow Trader Model}

Following the standard microstructure framing, order flow is split into
an informed arbitrageur and uninformed users (UU).

\subsubsection{Arbitrageur}
With fee fraction $f$ and $\gamma=1-f$, the pool price may sit anywhere
inside the no-arbitrage band $[P\gamma,\,P/\gamma]$ around the external
price $P$. Once per candle, after the retail flow, the arbitrageur moves
it to the nearest band edge if profitable
\emph{after gas}: with pool liquidity $L$, sqrt-price $s$ and target
$t$, the closed-form profit
\begin{equation}
  \Pi^{*}_{0\to1}=\frac{L(s-t)^{2}}{s},\qquad
  \Pi^{*}_{1\to0}=\frac{L(t-s)^{2}}{\gamma s},
  \label{eq:arb-profit}
\end{equation}
(derived in Sec.~\ref{app:arb}) must exceed $g\,p_{\mathrm{gas}}$,
where $g$ is the policy's measured
median gas and $p_{\mathrm{gas}}$ the scenario's gas price (5, 20, or
80~gwei), valued at the ETH price series. For the size-dependent
\texttt{MEVChargeHook} the optimum is found by ternary search; profit is
unimodal in size. $\Pi^{*}$ is a decision quantity only: all reported
metrics are computed from the balance changes of executed swaps.

\subsubsection{Uninformed users}
UU behavior follows the acceptance model of our prior work
\cite{lebedeva2025dynamic}: a candidate trade evaluates the normalized
outcome ratio
\begin{equation}
  r=\frac{V_{\mathrm{out}}-V_{\mathrm{in}}-G}
  {V_{\mathrm{out}}+V_{\mathrm{in}}+G},
  \label{eq:uu-r}
\end{equation}
where $V_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $V_{\mathrm{out}}$ are the two legs valued
at external prices, so fee \emph{and} slippage are inside $r$, and
$G$ is the trade's own gas. Execution is probabilistic,
\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{exec}}=
  \begin{cases}
    1, & r\ge 0,\\
    e^{-\lambda|r|}, & r<0,
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:uu-p}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=\ln 2/|r(30\,\mathrm{bps})|
\approx 461.4$, so that half of the willing flow declines at a 30~bps
total transaction cost. This calibration is what makes participation
fee-sensitive: at these trade sizes, the raw exponent
$e^{-|r|}$ is nearly flat across the fee box. The value of
$\lambda$ is a modeling assumption rather than a measured quantity and
is varied in sensitivity analysis.

Each minute $c$, each direction independently draws one candidate trade
of size
\begin{equation}
  q(c)=\frac{v_0(c)}{2}\;e^{\sigma Z-\sigma^{2}/2},
  \qquad Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1),\ \ \sigma=1,
  \label{eq:uu-size}
\end{equation}
where $v_0(c)$ is the minute's potential demand
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:uu-demand} below), split equally between the two
directions. The lognormal multiplier is mean-preserving
($\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma Z-\sigma^{2}/2}]=1$), so randomness produces
realistic heavy-tailed trade sizes without distorting the calibrated
volume; the trade then executes all-or-nothing with probability
$P_{\mathrm{exec}}$. Trades are discrete because they must displace the
pool price to create arbitrage opportunities, and each one carries its
own gas cost; all draws are pre-generated from a recorded seed, so the
randomness stays reproducible. One candidate trade per direction per
minute is the coarsest discretization of the measured volume.

\subsubsection{Demand calibration}
The minute-level potential demand $v_0(c)$ combines a measured intraday
shape with one assumed scale parameter, $\kappa$. With
$V_0(c),V_1(c)$ the 2024 per-minute Binance quote volumes of the
pair's two legs, the intraday shape is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{s}(c)=\frac{g(c)}
  {\overline{\sum_{c'\in\mathrm{day}}g(c')}},\qquad
  g(c)=\sqrt{V_0(c)\,V_1(c)},
  \label{eq:uu-shape}
\end{equation}
where the bar averages over the days of 2024, so $\hat{s}$ sums to one
over an average day, and
\begin{equation}
  v_{0}(c)=\kappa\cdot \mathrm{basket}\cdot\hat{s}(c),
  \label{eq:uu-demand}
\end{equation}
with the basket the pool's initial 20M~USDT deposit. Hence $\kappa$ is
the pool's daily turnover relative to its own TVL. The reference point
$\kappa=1$ is fixed by the parameterization, not by any result. The
experiment is
repeated at $\kappa\in\{0.03,0.1,0.3,1,3\}$, each a full matrix
never pooled with another, which moves the measured arbitrage share
of volume across $89\%,58\%,25\%,13\%,16\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:kappa},
Fig.~\ref{fig:demand}).

\subsection{Design and Statistics}

One operating point is a full factorial over 9 policy configurations
(the five hook policies plus static baselines at 5/30/60/100~bps),
times 3 pairs, 72 windows, and 3 gas scenarios: 5{,}832 cells. Five
operating points give 29{,}160 cells.
Figure~\ref{fig:fee-response} illustrates the policies' qualitative
behavior on one high-volatility day: directional policies widen the
fee against the market and narrow the opposite side; the constant-sum
policy shifts little; \texttt{VolatilityHook} rises to the fee ceiling
as volatility increases; \texttt{MEVChargeHook} alternates between its
base and capped rate.
The LP outcome of a cell is
$\mathrm{net} = R_f - IL = -L_{\mathrm{net}}$, the negative of the
fee-adjusted loss of Eq.~\eqref{eq:netloss}: the LP's
position and accrued fees against simply holding the deposit, all
marked at end-of-window prices.

Every comparison is \emph{paired}: a policy's window result is
differenced against the pre-declared 30~bps static baseline on the same
window, pair, and gas price, removing the day-to-day variation that
dwarfs policy effects. Effects are reported as the median paired
difference with a 95\% percentile-bootstrap confidence interval
(10{,}000 seeded resamples), written median $[$lower, upper$]$
throughout; a comparison counts as a win or loss only
if the interval excludes zero. Headline
numbers aggregate cell-first (one median per pair$\times$regime$\times$gas
cell, then across cells), so three gas scenarios of one day are never
treated as independent observations.
A post-hoc comparison against the best static fee in the box (60~bps) is
reported and labeled exploratory.

\subsubsection{Aggregate performance}
Table~\ref{tab:headline} reports the primary comparison at the
reference point: no evaluated dynamic policy beats the static 30~bps
baseline; four lose decisively and \texttt{BAHook} is indistinguishable
from it. The same holds at every other operating point: pooled across
regimes and gas scenarios, no hook's interval excludes zero at any
$\kappa$. Among the static levels themselves, both boundary fees lose
to 30~bps decisively while 60~bps does not, a plateau at 30--60~bps
consistent with the model's predicted revenue-maximizing fee of
$f^{*}\approx 2/\lambda\approx 43$~bps.

Decomposing the paired differences shows that the impermanent-loss
term is policy-invariant: it differs by single-digit USDT across
policies, against fee-income effects in the hundreds. Arbitrage pins
the end-of-window pool price to the external price under every policy,
so adaptation cannot reduce adverse selection in this model; it can
only price the flow, and it prices it slightly worse than a well-chosen
constant while paying its own gas premium on top.

\subsubsection{Conditional performance}
Splitting results by both experimental axes together shows a
consistent pattern (Fig.~\ref{fig:grid}): every policy's advantage over
the baseline improves monotonically with volatility and degrades
monotonically with gas, in every regime and at every $\kappa$. At the
reference point ($\kappa{=}1$), \texttt{BAHook} beats the baseline by
$+1{,}556\;[+894,+2{,}501]$ USDT per window in high-volatility windows
at 5~gwei, but loses or ties at 80~gwei. Which policy carries this
advantage shifts with the flow mix (Fig.~\ref{fig:kappa-trend-high}):
\texttt{BAHook} at retail-dominated $\kappa$, \texttt{VolatilityHook}
and \texttt{DAHook} at informed-heavy $\kappa$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:grid},
bottom half of each cell).

These conditional findings are stable across random seeds: a five-seed
replication of a 24-window subset preserves the sign of every
conditional finding (Fig.~\ref{fig:seed-robustness}), and
\texttt{ABHook} stays significantly negative at nearly every seed,
showing the procedure also detects real disadvantages.

\subsubsection{Gas overhead}
The gas gradient follows directly from the policies' own overhead.
Oracle-reading hooks cost 108--124k gas per swap against the static
hook's 76k, and that difference enters retail's acceptance test through
$G$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:uu-r}. At 80~gwei even the static hook's
${\sim}\$19$ per swap amounts to ${\sim}47$~bps of a typical \$4{,}000
retail trade, so transaction costs rival the fee box itself. The
informed share of volume rises with gas accordingly, from 12.4\% to
13.7\% across 5$\to$80~gwei at $\kappa{=}1$, as small retail trades are
priced out first.

Overall, none of the evaluated dynamic policies outperforms a
well-chosen static fee unconditionally. A reproducible conditional
advantage exists in high-volatility windows at low gas prices; which
policy carries it depends on the informed share of flow, and the
advantage is consumed by the policies' own gas premium as gas rises.
For the framework, what matters here is that this conditional
structure could be measured at all: paired, seed-checked, and
decomposed along pre-declared axes, with every policy running through
the same implementation path.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}

The declared model limits: retail has no strategy and no correlated
demand; one arbitrageur acts once per candle with perfect information
and no mempool competition; liquidity is passive and full-range; mock
feeds validate integration, not live oracle behavior; the retail scale
$\kappa$ is swept over two orders of magnitude, not calibrated to real
pools; retail lumpiness is fixed at one trade per
direction per minute; results cover one year, three pairs, and one
venue's data. The main matrix uses one recorded seed; the five-seed
replication of Fig.~\ref{fig:seed-robustness} covers a window subset
rather than the full matrix. The conditional corner finding was not
pre-registered and is reported as observed structure; the unconditional
findings and the failed-and-reported gas prediction follow the
pre-registered protocol.

\section{Derivation of the Arbitrage Optimum}
\label{app:arb}

Within one tick range a Uniswap v3/v4 pool with liquidity $L$ obeys the
constant-product amount relations in sqrt-price coordinates: moving the
pool price from $\sqrt{p}=s_1$ to $s_2$ exchanges
\begin{equation}
  \Delta y = L\,(s_2-s_1),\qquad
  \Delta x = L\left(\frac{1}{s_1}-\frac{1}{s_2}\right),
  \label{eq:app-amounts}
\end{equation}
and the LP fee $f$ ($\gamma=1-f$) is charged on the input amount before
it enters the curve.

\subsubsection{Band edges}
Selling token0 (zero-for-one) at pool sqrt-price $s$ realizes a marginal
price $\gamma s^{2}$ in token1 per token0; the trade is profitable
against the external price $P$ while $\gamma s^{2}>P$, i.e. while
$s>\sqrt{P/\gamma}=t_{0\to1}$. Buying token0 costs $s^{2}/\gamma$
marginally and is profitable while $s^{2}/\gamma<P$, i.e. while
$s<\sqrt{P\gamma}=t_{1\to0}$. Hence the no-arbitrage band
$[P\gamma,\,P/\gamma]$ on the pool price, and the profit-maximizing
trade stops exactly at the near edge: the integrand of marginal profit
changes sign there, so profit is unimodal in trade size with its
maximum at the edge.

\subsubsection{Sell side}
Moving the pool from $s$ down to $t=t_{0\to1}$ requires a net token0
input $\Delta x=L(1/t-1/s)$, for which the trader pays the grossed-up
$\Delta x/\gamma$, and yields $\Delta y=L(s-t)$ of token1. Valued at the
external price $P=\gamma t^{2}$,
\begin{align}
  \Pi_{0\to1}
  &= L(s-t)-\frac{P}{\gamma}\,L\!\left(\frac{1}{t}-\frac{1}{s}\right)
  = L(s-t)-L\,t^{2}\,\frac{s-t}{ts}\nonumber\\
  &= L(s-t)\!\left(1-\frac{t}{s}\right)
  = \frac{L\,(s-t)^{2}}{s}.
  \label{eq:app-sell}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Buy side}
Moving the pool from $s$ up to $t=t_{1\to0}$ requires net token1 input
$L(t-s)$, paid as $L(t-s)/\gamma$, and yields
$\Delta x=L(1/s-1/t)$ of token0 worth $P\,\Delta x$. With
$P=t^{2}/\gamma$,
\begin{align}
  \Pi_{1\to0}
  &= \frac{t^{2}}{\gamma}\,L\,\frac{t-s}{ts}-\frac{L(t-s)}{\gamma}
  = \frac{L(t-s)}{\gamma}\!\left(\frac{t}{s}-1\right)\nonumber\\
  &= \frac{L\,(t-s)^{2}}{\gamma\,s}.
  \label{eq:app-buy}
\end{align}
The extra $1/\gamma$ relative to Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-sell} arises because
the fee is charged in token1, the numeraire the profit is measured in,
so, unlike the sell side, it does not cancel against the external
valuation of the input.

\subsubsection{Gas}
Gas adds a size-independent cost $g\,p_{\mathrm{gas}}$, which shifts the
profit curve down without moving its maximum; checking
$\Pi^{*}>g\,p_{\mathrm{gas}}$ after the size optimization is therefore
equivalent to including gas inside it (the arbitrageur cannot split a
trade across transactions in this model). For a size-dependent fee the
closed form is replaced by a ternary search, valid because an input fee
non-decreasing in size preserves unimodality.
